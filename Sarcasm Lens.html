What does this website do?

It's the live demo of my sarcasm-detection project. Sarcasm says one thing and means the opposite, so computers often get fooled. Here you can watch two models try.

1. You type

Write any headline or sentence, or tap one of the ready-made examples.

2. Two models read it

Logistic Regression adds up word clues. DistilBERT reads the whole sentence in context.

3. You see why

Each model gives a sarcasm meter, a plain-English reason, and highlighted clue words.

Try the detector

Pick a view, type something, press Analyze. Pink means sarcastic, blue means sincere.

Meet the two models

Same data, same test: 28,503 headlines from The Onion (sarcastic) and HuffPost (real).

Logistic Regression

The word-weigher. Every word has a score (“area” leans sarcastic, “trump” leans real) and the scores are added up. Simple, and you can see exactly why it answered.

DistilBERT

The context reader. A small Transformer that already knows English and was fine-tuned on our headlines. It understands word order, but it's a black box.

Good to know

DistilBERT scores about 92% on headlines, but on everyday sarcasm like “I absolutely love being stuck in traffic” it caught only 4 of 12 sarcastic texts in my stress test. The dataset's labels are really which newspaper wrote it, so the models mostly learned The Onion's writing style, not sarcasm itself. Try the “everyday” examples above to see it happen.
